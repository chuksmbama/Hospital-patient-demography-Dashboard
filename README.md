## Project overview 
This patient demography dashboard gives hospital leadership a clear view of who their patients are, the conditions they are treated for, and how they rate their care experience. It supports more informed resource-allocation decisions, helps surface gaps in service quality, and strengthens long-term operational planning.


## Data cleaning and preparation 
- Used DAX expression to calculate the total number of patients, male patients, female patients, and the average treatment cost.
- Categorised and grouped the ages of the patients into groups of 10s.

## Data Visualization 
- KPI cards for total patients, male/female patients count, and average treatment cost
- Age group distribution of patients (vertical bar chart)
- Ratio of recovered to stable patients (doughnut chart)
- patient satisfaction rating (horizontal bar chart)
- Data table for conditions, procedures, patient volumes and treatment cost
- Slicer for gender

## Insights 
- The 50-59 age group (262 patients) and the 60-69 age group (277 patients) represent the largest segment, totalling 66.6% of all patients, while the other age groups make up 33.4% of all patients.
- More female patients (53.25%) compared to male patients (46.75%), with an almost even distribution between genders.
- There are more elderly male patients compared to female patients
- Most patients gave a 3.00 rating (458 responses), meaning the service is seen as acceptable, not great. A large group of 394 patients rated the service 2.00 or 1.00, showing they were not satisfied. The highest rating of 4.00 was only given by 132 patients, which confirms that we have to improve the service from acceptable to excellent.
- 591 patients (60.6%) have achieved full recovery; however, 393 patients (39.94%) are in stable conditions. This shows treatment is effective.
- Male patients had a 5.43% higher stability rate than females, while females had a 5.43% higher recovery rate.
- The number of patients with a particular condition is very similar, with most conditions having about 65 patients. This shows that the patient mix is quite balanced across the different health issues we track.
- The average treatment cost for female patients is about 74.70% higher than the average cost for male patients.

## Recomendations
-Most patients see our service as simply “okay”, and many are dissatisfied. We need to dig into the feedback behind these low ratings by reviewing patient comments, running short follow-ups and acting quickly on recurring complaints.
- Older adults make up the majority of our patients, so our facilities, equipment and staff training should be tailored to the conditions and care needs of this group.
- A major driver of higher female treatment costs is childbirth. We should review our billing model to ensure pricing is fair, competitive and aligned with market standards.
- To improve our recovery rate, we need stronger follow-through. This includes ensuring patients stick to their medication schedules, complete treatment cycles and receive timely follow-up calls or digital reminders.
- Because patient numbers are balanced across conditions, staffing, supplies and specialist support should also be evenly allocated to avoid gaps in care.

## Summary
We can improve our recovery outcomes by speeding up intervention, improving care coordination and giving patients stronger support after treatment. Earlier diagnosis, closer tracking of high-risk patients and consistent follow-up plans will help more patients move from stable to fully recovered. Addressing the gaps between male and female recovery patterns and improving patient education will further raise recovery rates and strengthen overall treatment effectiveness.
